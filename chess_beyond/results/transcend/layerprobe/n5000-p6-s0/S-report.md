# S: how much each layer says about its own future moves

5000 positions; S's greedy continuation for 6 plies (both colours); targets: from-square, to-square and piece of m1 (its move here), m3 and m5 (its next two), and check / capture by the side to move within the plies. Multinomial logistic probes from the activations at the position (the token predicting m1), L2 by validation; test cross-entropy in nats. gain = CE(baseline) - CE(layer), 95% bootstrap intervals over test games. Baselines: board; board + next move (m1's from- and to-square added); board + next move + history (also the last 6 plies before the position, one-hot from- and to-square each: the prompt is the whole game, and recent moves predict later ones).

## The chosen layer, corrected control: Lfinal

The first rule (below) used a board-matched swap that could not discriminate: its stand-ins were 17.2 one-hot entries from the real board, further than a line's own board 4 plies earlier (7.6), and every layer passed it. Corrected rule, fixed before its results: residuals after a small MLP (two hidden layers of 512) on the board and the last 6 plies at the same ply, so neither nonlinear board information nor recent history stays in them; a layer passes if its own-line autocorrelation at lag 4 is significantly above the board-matched swap counted only where the stand-in is at least as close to the real board as the line's own earlier board (or above the random swap if fewer than 200 test lines have such a match), and at least half the maximum over layers. The latest passing layer is chosen.

## The first rule's choice: Lfinal

Rule (fixed before the results): a layer passes if its board-removed residual autocorrelation at lag 4 is significantly above the board-matched swap (the 95% bootstrap interval over test lines of own - matched above zero) and at least half the maximum over layers. The latest passing layer is chosen.

- Maximum residual autocorrelation over layers 0.476; half of it 0.238.
- Above the board-matched swap: L0, L1, L2, L3, L4, L5, L6, L7, L8, L9, L10, L11, L12, L13, L14, L15, Lfinal.
- Passing (both): L2, L8, L9, L10, L11, L12, L13, L14, L15, Lfinal.
- Chosen: Lfinal, the latest passing layer.

Persistence: residual = the state minus its ridge prediction from the board at the same ply (fit on the train positions), standardised per dimension; the correlation of the residual at ply t with ply t + 4 of the same greedy line (own), pooled over dimensions and t, on test lines. Against three levels: random swap (ply t + 4 from a random other test position: the chance floor); board-matched swap (the state at the position (pool: all), from a different game, whose board is nearest to the real ply t + 4 board: on average 17.2 one-hot entries apart, against 7.6 between a line's own plies t and t + 4; the board control, since the residual keeps any nonlinear board information, which persists as slowly as the board does); and the raw board's own autocorrelation (the same statistic on the standardised one-hot board): 0.560, board-matched 0.119. Caveat: the prompt is the whole game, so a residual also carries the game's history, which persists along a line whatever the model plans; the board-matched swap comes from another game and does not remove that.
